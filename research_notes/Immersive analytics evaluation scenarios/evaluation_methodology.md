# Evaluation Methodology for Geospatial / Urban Immersive Analytics (VR vs Desktop 3D)

Scope: how comparable immersive analytics (IA) and 3D-map studies design tasks, conditions, measures and procedures, so the Helsingborg digital twin (Unity; desktop 3D works, Quest 3S VR planned; choropleth / extrusion / glyph encodings on SCB 250 m and 1 km grid cells; picking + info panel; preset switching; A/B block compare) can get a sound, feasible usability study (n ~ 12-24, 45-90 min).

Source-verification note: items marked "(verified)" were read in full text during this session (Tilt Map, Ens et al., Wagner Filho et al., Besançon et al., Kraus et al. survey, UrbanVR, Wang et al. CISBAT). Classic taxonomy/instrument citations marked "(DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched)" are standard references whose bibliographic details are well established, but the DOI link was not opened in this session; check them before final thesis submission.

## 1. Task taxonomies and how studies turn them into benchmark tasks with verifiable answers

### Takeaway
Comparable geo-IA studies do not invent tasks freely: they pick a small number (1-7) of tasks from cartographic task taxonomies (Andrienko & Andrienko elementary vs synoptic; Roth's objectives Identify / Compare / Rank / Associate / Delineate; Amar et al. low-level tasks), then make each one answerable with a single checkable value (a numeric estimate, a chosen region, a ranking) and control difficulty by construction (answer ranges, near vs far targets, data variance). For the Helsingborg twin this maps directly to: retrieve value of a cell, compare two cells, find extreme cell in a district, estimate a district aggregate, and compare two blocks via the A/B feature.

### Cited Findings
- Tilt Map (Yang, Dwyer, Marriott, Jenny, Goodwin, IEEE TVCG 2020/2022, "Tilt Map: Interactive Transitions Between Choropleth Map, Prism Map and Bar Chart in Immersive Environments") explicitly grounds its tasks in the Andrienko & Andrienko "Tasks" chapter, Roth's interaction-primitive taxonomy and Yang & Goodwin's what-why analysis, using the distinction between **elementary** tasks (single elements) and **synoptic** tasks (a set of elements), and designs one task of each type (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
  - *Area-comparison task* (elementary): instead of a trivial binary "which is larger", participants **estimate the numeric difference** between two marked areas because binary answers were near ceiling accuracy in all conditions (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
  - *Region task* (synoptic): **estimate the (average) density of a region of contiguous marked areas**; answers given as an integer 0-100 on a slider; error = absolute difference from the true value ("AbsDiff") (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
  - Difficulty control: comparison targets split into "close" vs "far" geographic distance; region-task variance fixed via coefficient of variation 40-60%; correct answers balanced across 20-40 / 40-60 / 60-80 ranges; targets highlighted so search time is excluded (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- Besançon, Cooper, Ynnerman & Vernier ("An Evaluation of Visualization Methods for Population Statistics Based on Choropleth Maps", IEEE TVCG, 2021; data/scripts at osf.io/8rxwg) built tasks from **Roth's taxonomy** (operands Space-Alone / Attributes-in-Space / Space-in-Time; objectives Identify, Compare, Rank, Associate, Delineate) and added a **Summarize** objective that Roth lacks; tasks: Q1 read population of a region, Q2 read unemployment of a region, Q3 combine both to find the region with most unemployed people, Q4 identify the neighbour with most unemployed, Q5 average unemployed over multiple regions and compare with another average (verified) — [OSF repository osf.io/8rxwg](https://osf.io/8rxwg/)
- Wagner Filho, Stuerzlinger & Nedel (IEEE TVCG 26(1), 2020, "Evaluating an Immersive Space-Time Cube Geovisualization for Intuitive Trajectory Data Exploration") used **7 tasks** each classified by target movement feature (instant position, meeting time, instant distance, movement speed, order of events, etc.) and difficulty, e.g., "Where was Person A at 10am on the 8th?", "Who moved the fastest after leaving home on the 6th?" (verified) — [arXiv 1908.00580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00580)
- Yang et al. "Maps and Globes in Virtual Reality" (Computer Graphics Forum 37(3), EuroVis 2018) used three perceptual geo tasks — **distance comparison, area comparison, direction estimation** — each at controlled difficulty levels, 9 repetitions each — [arXiv 1908.02088](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02088)
- The Kraus et al. IA survey (Computer Graphics Forum 41(1):201-229, 2022, 83 proceedings across 8 conferences 1990-2020) found the IA literature's tasks cluster into clustering/classification, anomaly (outlier) detection (rarely studied: "only two" papers deploy an outlier task), pattern/trend analysis, visual search, and comparison; screen-vs-immersive comparisons usually measure "distance estimation, cluster segregation, and outlier detection" (verified) — [Kraus et al. 2022, Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14430)
- UrbanVR (Zhang, Zeng, Liu, "UrbanVR: An immersive analytics system for context-aware urban design", Computers & Graphics 2021) derived analytical tasks with a collaborating architect via Munzner's nested model, then evaluated an **interaction** task (select a building candidate and manipulate it to match a target position/orientation/size) rather than a data-reading task (verified) — [arXiv 2107.00227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00227)
- Wang et al. (CISBAT 2025, J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 3140 082017, "Enhancing urban digital twin interfaces to support thermal comfort planning") used **three tasks of increasing complexity** on a web urban digital twin: retrieve data from a specific weather station (lookup), identify hot spots on microclimate maps (find extremes / pattern), and synthesize survey responses with measured climate on thermal walks (integrate/associate) (verified) — [Wang et al. 2025 PDF](https://ual.sg/publication/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces.pdf)
- Classic taxonomies to cite when justifying task choice (DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched):
  - Amar, Eagan & Stasko (2005), "Low-level components of analytic activity in information visualization", IEEE InfoVis 2005 — 10 low-level tasks: retrieve value, filter, compute derived value, find extremum, sort, determine range, characterize distribution, find anomalies, cluster, correlate — [doi:10.1109/INFVIS.2005.1532136](https://doi.org/10.1109/INFVIS.2005.1532136)
  - Brehmer & Munzner (2013), "A multi-level typology of abstract visualization tasks", IEEE TVCG 19(12) — why (consume/produce; search: lookup/browse/locate/explore; query: identify/compare/summarize) / how / what — [doi:10.1109/TVCG.2013.124](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2013.124)
  - Roth (2013), "An empirically-derived taxonomy of interaction primitives for interactive cartography and geovisualization", IEEE TVCG 19(12) — [doi:10.1109/TVCG.2013.130](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2013.130)
  - Andrienko & Andrienko (2006), *Exploratory Analysis of Spatial and Temporal Data: A Systematic Approach*, Springer (elementary vs synoptic; lookup / comparison / relation-seeking) — [doi:10.1007/3-540-31190-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-31190-4)

### Inferences
- A defensible task set for the Helsingborg twin, each with a single verifiable answer computable from the pipeline outputs (one per row of a task table in the thesis):
  1. **Lookup / retrieve value (elementary, Identify)** — "What is the population of the highlighted 250 m cell?" (answer from picking + info panel; tests picking, panel, legend reading).
  2. **Compare two cells (elementary, Compare)** — "Which of cells A and B has the higher median income, and by roughly how much?" Use numeric-difference estimation (Tilt Map) to avoid ceiling effects; vary near vs far and same vs different encoding.
  3. **Find extreme (synoptic, Rank)** — "Within district X, which cell has the highest value?" (tests overview + occlusion handling of extrusions).
  4. **Summarize region (synoptic, Summarize)** — "Estimate the average value over the outlined statistical area" (Besançon Q5 / Tilt Map region task).
  5. **Associate / correlate two layers** — "In the outlined area, do cells with tall buildings also have high income?" (multivariate; Besançon found such combination tasks have only ~50-65% accuracy, so expect difficulty).
  6. **Block compare (A/B)** — "Using the compare tool, which block has more residents per building / larger difference in X?" (tests the copy & compare feature; answer from difference table).
  7. Optional open-ended exploration for insight counting (see section 2).
- Keep targets highlighted for performance tasks (as Tilt Map and Besançon did) so time measures reading/judging, not visual search across 44,000 buildings; include one separate "locate" task if navigation itself is a research question.
- Use 0-100 normalized legends or exact-value panels consistently so absolute error is comparable across encodings.

### Gaps
- No IA-specific task taxonomy dedicated to urban digital twins was found in this session; the Ens et al. challenge C15 ("Defining Application Scenarios") says the field still lacks guidance on which tasks benefit from immersion (see section 5).
- Did not retrieve the Brehmer-Munzner or Amar et al. full texts this session; definitions above are from standard knowledge.

## 2. Typical study designs and which are feasible for a master thesis

### Takeaway
The dominant design in geo-IA is a **within-subjects controlled lab study, 12-32 participants, ~1-1.5 h, 2-4 conditions, Latin-square counterbalanced, with different but equivalent data per trial**, plus post-condition questionnaires and a short interview. Exploratory/insight-based sessions and expert feedback are used as complements (n = 2-10). For a single student, the most feasible core is either (a) VR vs desktop on the same tasks (2 conditions, within-subjects) or (b) encoding comparison (choropleth vs extrusion vs glyph) within one medium — not both crossed fully unless trials are very short.

### Cited Findings
- **Tilt Map Study 1**: within-subjects, 3 visualisations (choropleth, monochrome prism, coloured prism) x 2 tasks x 2 datasets x 3 answer ranges x 2 repetitions = **72 responses/participant, ~1.5 h**, **12 participants** (university students/researchers, aged 20-30; 7 had <5 h VR experience), HTC Vive Pro, map placed 0.6 m in front, 1 x 1 m, tilted 45°, prism heights mapped to 0-20 cm; Latin square order; 5-minute break between tasks (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- **Tilt Map Study 2**: **18 new participants**, 3 conditions (Tilt Map, Side-by-Side, Toggle) x 1 task x 2 datasets x 3 answer ranges x 2 reps = **36 responses/participant, ~1 h 10 min**; added a pre-study VR training (5-10 min) because a pilot with a VR-naive participant revealed initial difficulty with VR interactions (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- **Wagner Filho et al. space-time cube**: **mixed design**, 2 within-subjects conditions (desktop vs immersive) x 2 between-subjects data scenarios (simple vs dense) x 7 tasks = **14 trials/participant, average 65 minutes** incl. training; **20 participants** (16 m / 4 f, mean age 22.2); authors note 65 min for 14 trials "can be considered very long" (verified) — [arXiv 1908.00580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00580)
- **Yang et al. Maps and Globes in VR**: within-subjects, **32 participants**, 4 visualisations x 3 tasks x 9 repetitions = 108 questions/participant, ~1 hour — [arXiv 1908.02088](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02088)
- **Kraus et al. 2020** ("The Impact of Immersion on Cluster Identification Tasks", IEEE TVCG 26(1)): within-subjects, **18 participants**, 4 conditions: 2D scatterplot matrix on screen, 3D scatterplot on screen, 3D miniature in VR, fully immersive 3D scatterplot in VR — [KOPS Konstanz record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/93ac6436-a84c-4f9a-bfec-20cba8a52b88)
- **UrbanVR**: within-subjects, 4 interaction modes x 3 scene complexities = 12 conditions, **15 participants**, seated, 5 min intro + 10 min training, ~1.5 h; plus **expert interviews with 2 architects** who explored freely for ~20 min (verified) — [arXiv 2107.00227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00227)
- **Wang et al. CISBAT 2025 (urban digital twin usability)**: **10 domain participants** (5 Singapore, 5 USA; planners/researchers), **60-minute** one-to-one sessions: background questions -> **5-min free exploration** -> 3 guided tasks -> survey (UEQ) -> open discussion; responses published on GitHub (verified) — [Wang et al. 2025 PDF](https://ual.sg/publication/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces.pdf)
- **Besançon et al.** used a two-stage approach: a qualitative online pre-study with visualization researchers to prune candidate techniques, then a **preregistered online experiment** (58 valid participants; second experiment 47), technique order counterbalanced, **4 equivalent question sets** rotated across techniques to avoid learning effects, **no interaction allowed** (no rotate/zoom) to remove confounds — authors acknowledge this "does not reflect the real usage" and could disadvantage 3D (verified) — [OSF osf.io/8rxwg](https://osf.io/8rxwg/)
- **Insight-based evaluation**: Saraiya, North & Duca (IEEE TVCG 11(4), 2005, "An insight-based methodology for evaluating bioinformatics visualizations") evaluated five microarray tools in open-ended sessions by counting and characterizing insights (amount, type, time to insight), arguing benchmark tasks miss real exploratory value — [PDF (Stasko course mirror)](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~john.stasko/8001/saraiya05.pdf)
- Kraus et al. survey classifies IA evaluation papers into (i) assessing suitability of a visualization in AR/VR, (ii) human-factors studies of immersion, (iii) **comparing media (screen vs immersive)**; the scatterplot is the most frequently compared visualization; only one "model" paper on how to evaluate IA (Billow & Cottam 2017, heuristic evaluation) (verified) — [Kraus et al. 2022](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14430)
- Seven-scenarios framework for choosing an evaluation type (e.g., user performance UP, user experience UX, visual data analysis and reasoning VDAR, collaborative data analysis CDA): Lam, Bertini, Isenberg, Plaisant & Carpendale (2012), "Empirical Studies in Information Visualization: Seven Scenarios", IEEE TVCG 18(9) (DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched) — [doi:10.1109/TVCG.2011.279](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2011.279)

### Inferences
- Feasible core design for this thesis (fits 60-75 min, n = 16-24):
  - **Option A (medium comparison):** 2 conditions (Desktop 3D vs Quest 3S VR), within-subjects, counterbalanced order (AB/BA, half each), 5-6 task types x 2 repetitions per condition with different but equivalent targets per condition (parallel task sets, rotated), fixed encoding preset per task. ~12 trials per condition. Matches Wagner Filho's size (20 participants, 14 trials, 65 min).
  - **Option B (encoding comparison, if VR is late):** 3 encodings (choropleth / extruded columns / glyph) on desktop only, within-subjects, Latin square — directly replicates Tilt Map Study 1 (n = 12, 3 conditions) on a real city grid.
  - Full crossing (2 media x 3 encodings x 5 tasks x reps) would exceed 90 min given Tilt Map's 72 trials took 1.5 h; if both factors are needed, make encoding a between-task factor or reduce to 2 encodings.
- Add a short **free-exploration phase with think-aloud** (5-10 min, as in Wang et al.) at the end, and count insights per Saraiya/North as a qualitative complement; this exercises preset switching and block compare in an ecologically valid way.
- Recruit 2-3 domain users (planners at Helsingborg municipality / Gothenburg-area researchers) for an expert walkthrough, mirroring UrbanVR's and Wang et al.'s expert sessions, addressing the Ens et al. critique of student-only samples.
- Always run 1-2 pilots with VR-naive participants (Tilt Map learned this the hard way) and add a standardized VR interaction training scene.

### Gaps
- No published study found that compares exactly desktop-3D vs HMD for a **building-level city digital twin with statistical grid layers**; closest analogues are prism/choropleth VR studies (abstract maps) and urban-design VR systems (UrbanVR) or web UDT usability tests (Wang et al.).
- Power analysis numbers for VR-vs-desktop effects were not found; sample sizes in the field are convention-driven (12-32).

## 3. Standard measures/instruments and known pitfalls

### Takeaway
Core measures in comparable studies: task completion time, accuracy (binary correct or absolute error), SUS, NASA (Raw) TLX, a simulator sickness questionnaire (SSQ pre/post), preference rankings and confidence Likert ratings, plus logged head/controller/map pose for behavioural analysis; UEQ is used in UDT usability work; presence (IPQ) is optional. Main pitfalls: learning effects (handled by counterbalancing + different equivalent data per trial), VR unfamiliarity/novelty bias, fatigue in long sessions, cybersickness, and confounds from free interaction.

### Cited Findings
- **Time + error**: Tilt Map measured time from first render to double-click of controller trigger, then answer via slider; error = absolute difference to correct answer; also logged headset, controller and map position/rotation **every 0.1 s**, enabling analysis of view angle (participants spent significantly more time at tilt angles > 45° with prism maps, interpreted as fighting occlusion) (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- **Preference/confidence**: Tilt Map post-hoc questionnaire: preference ranking (visual design, ease of use), 5-point confidence Likert per visualisation, advantages/disadvantages, strategies, demographics; analysed with Friedman tests; accuracy/time with linear mixed models + Tukey HSD (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- **SUS + NASA Raw TLX + SSQ**: Wagner Filho et al. administered SUS, NASA Raw TLX and the Simulator Sickness Questionnaire after each condition; SUS desktop **62.1 (SD 20.6)** vs immersive **82.3 (SD 10.1)**, significant; Raw TLX overall 31.6 vs 26.8 (p = .07, n.s.) but Mental Workload and Frustration significantly lower in VR; pre- vs post-VR SSQ difference not significant (p = .22, mean delta 2.8, SD 16.5, "negligible"); no discomfort in ~25-min VR sessions; interaction logs showed many more camera movements in VR (verified) — [arXiv 1908.00580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00580)
- **UEQ in urban digital twins**: Wang et al. report UEQ attractiveness **1.67**, stimulation **1.70**, perspicuity **0.75** (low) for the BEAM web UDT — i.e., UDTs can look attractive while being hard to understand (verified) — [Wang et al. 2025 PDF](https://ual.sg/publication/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces.pdf)
- **Effect sizes and CIs instead of p-values**: Besançon et al. report bootstrap 95% CIs and pairwise differences/ratios, citing APA recommendations and NHST criticism; preregistered exclusion criteria (verified) — [OSF osf.io/8rxwg](https://osf.io/8rxwg/)
- **Learning effects / counterbalancing**: Tilt Map used Latin-square ordering and "different data for each question" to minimise learning; Besançon used 4 equivalent question sets rotated across techniques (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120); [OSF osf.io/8rxwg](https://osf.io/8rxwg/)
- **Novelty bias, fatigue, nausea**: Ens et al. (CHI 2021) C16 warns that IA studies often use "short experiments with participants that are easily accessible" (students/colleagues), that IA amplifies "nausea, fatigue, and physical discomfort", and that novelty causes "short term bias towards over-acceptance"; calls for short- and long-term assessment accommodating novelty bias and learning effects (verified) — [Ens et al. 2021, NSF PAR PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10290646)
- Kraus et al. survey: interaction difficulties are often cited as hindering immersive analysis; unfamiliarity of VR/AR input is a common justification for poor VR results, and training may improve effectiveness but reduce excitement/engagement (verified) — [Kraus et al. 2022](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14430)
- UrbanVR: participants seated, no dizziness reported in 10-min trials (one reported eye dryness); experts said gestures "are not comparable with mouse regarding accuracy" (verified) — [arXiv 2107.00227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00227)
- Instrument sources (DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched):
  - SUS: Brooke (1996), "SUS: A quick and dirty usability scale", in *Usability Evaluation in Industry*, Taylor & Francis (cited as such in Wagner Filho et al. [verified reference list] — [arXiv 1908.00580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00580)); interpretation norms: Bangor, Kortum & Miller (2008), "An empirical evaluation of the System Usability Scale", IJHCI 24(6) — [doi:10.1080/10447310802205776](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447310802205776)
  - NASA-TLX: Hart & Staveland (1988) in *Human Mental Workload*; Hart (2006) "NASA-Task Load Index (NASA-TLX); 20 years later", HFES Annual Meeting 50 (cited in Wagner Filho et al., verified reference list) — [arXiv 1908.00580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00580)
  - SSQ: Kennedy, Lane, Berbaum & Lilienthal (1993), "Simulator Sickness Questionnaire: An enhanced method for quantifying simulator sickness", Int. J. Aviation Psychology 3(3):203-220 — [doi:10.1207/s15327108ijap0303_3](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327108ijap0303_3)
  - VRSQ (shorter, HMD-oriented SSQ variant): Kim, Park, Choi & Choe (2018), "Virtual reality sickness questionnaire (VRSQ)", Applied Ergonomics 69:66-73 — [doi:10.1016/j.apergo.2017.12.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apergo.2017.12.016)
  - IPQ (presence): Schubert, Friedmann & Regenbrecht (2001), "The experience of presence: Factor analytic insights", Presence 10(3):266-281 — [doi:10.1162/105474601300343603](https://doi.org/10.1162/105474601300343603); questionnaire at [igroup.org](http://www.igroup.org/pq/ipq/index.php)
  - UEQ: Laugwitz, Held & Schrepp (2008), "Construction and evaluation of a user experience questionnaire", USAB 2008, LNCS 5298 — [doi:10.1007/978-3-540-89350-9_6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-89350-9_6)

### Inferences
- Recommended measurement package (per condition, ~8 min of questionnaires): SUS (10 items) + NASA Raw TLX (6 items) + per-condition confidence Likert; SSQ or VRSQ **before the first VR exposure and after each VR block** (also after desktop if you want a symmetric baseline); end-of-session preference ranking + 10-min semi-structured interview. Add UEQ-S (8 items) only if session time allows; IPQ only if presence is a research question (for a usability thesis, likely optional).
- Implement interaction logging **before** the study (currently not implemented): per trial log session ID, condition, task ID, start/answer timestamps, answer, correctness, camera/head pose at 10 Hz, picked entity IDs, preset switches, compare-tool use. This is required both for time measurement and for behavioural findings like Tilt Map's view-angle analysis and Wagner Filho's interaction counts.
- Keep VR blocks under ~25-30 min continuous (Wagner Filho's no-discomfort result was at ~25 min), seated, with snap/teleport or table-top map scaling rather than smooth flying to minimise cybersickness; break between conditions.
- Report means with 95% CIs and effect sizes (Besançon) alongside non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon for 2 conditions, Friedman for 3) — n = 16-24 is too small for strong NHST claims.

### Gaps
- No eye-tracking data is collected in the comparable geo-IA studies read here; Quest 3S has no eye tracking, so head-gaze logging is the realistic proxy (inference).
- Did not find published SUS benchmarks specific to VR analytics tools beyond individual studies (e.g., 82.3 in Wagner Filho).

## 4. Findings from key comparable studies (3D/VR vs 2D maps, urban twins, navigation, collaboration)

### Takeaway
Across studies, **height (prism/extrusion) encoding is more accurate but slower than colour (choropleth)**, 3D brings **occlusion and perspective distortion** that users fight by moving/tilting the view, **objective performance VR vs desktop is often similar** while **subjective usability, preference and workload favour VR**, and multivariate "combine two variables" tasks are hard (~50-65% accuracy) regardless of encoding. Urban-twin usability studies mostly report qualitative issues (navigation, legend/interface clarity) with small expert samples.

### Cited Findings
- **Prism vs choropleth in VR (Tilt Map Study 1, n = 12)**: monochrome and coloured prism maps were **significantly more accurate** than choropleth for area-comparison (χ²(2) = 9.2, p = .0101), monochrome prism more accurate for region task (χ²(2) = 7.6, p = .0223); **choropleth significantly faster** (χ²(2) = 63.4, p < .0001); participants moved prism maps more and spent more time at large tilt angles (occlusion); 75% ranked coloured prism best for visual design and readability; more confidence with coloured prism (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- **Prior desktop/paper evidence** (as summarized in Tilt Map related work): Popelka's eye-tracking study found prism maps more accurate but slower than choropleth maps; Niedomysl et al. (printed gridded maps) found choropleth better for detailed ranking, prism slightly better for general patterns; Cuff & Bieri found readers associate prism **height, not volume**, with value; Bleisch, Dykes & Nebiker and Seipel & Carvalho found bar-height reading accuracy similar in 2D and 3D maps (verified via Tilt Map) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- **Hybrid views (Tilt Map Study 2, n = 18)**: Tilt Map significantly more accurate than side-by-side (χ²(2) = 6.5, p = .0393); toggle significantly slower than both; participants spent ~13.5% of time in intermediate choropleth-prism states (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- **3D choropleth (extrusion) on desktop, large online study (Besançon et al., n = 58 and 47)**: 3D choropleths were the **most frequently ranked favourite** and performed as well as alternatives; evidence 3D choropleths could be faster for reading population (Q1); accuracy was poor for tasks combining two variables (~50% Q3, ~65% Q4); popchart 3D prism maps performed worst on region averaging (Q4Sum) in time and accuracy, with participants complaining height was hard to compare (verified) — [OSF osf.io/8rxwg](https://osf.io/8rxwg/)
- **Immersive vs desktop space-time cube (Wagner Filho, n = 20)**: quantitative performance similar for most tasks (basic instant-position task slightly slower in VR); clear advantages for VR in SUS (82.3 vs 62.1), lower mental workload and frustration, preference (19/20 more engaging, 18/20 more intuitive) (verified) — [arXiv 1908.00580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00580)
- **Maps and globes (Yang et al. 2018, n = 32)**: 3D globes more effective than flat projections for conveying distance and direction (as summarized by Ens et al.) — [Ens et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10290646); study design at [arXiv 1908.02088](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02088)
- **Clusters in VR (Kraus et al. 2020, n = 18)**: multi-dimensional clusters easier to identify in VR than on 2D desktop small multiples (summarized by Ens et al.) — [Ens et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10290646)
- Ens et al. synthesis: "there appears to be tangible benefit to immersive display when the data is inherently more than two-dimensional"; Kwon et al. found immersive VR graph layouts gave faster decisions and fewer errors than 2D (verified) — [Ens et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10290646)
- **Urban design VR (UrbanVR, n = 15 + 2 experts)**: handle-bar and viewpoint-optimization interaction modes improved building manipulation time vs plain gestures; experts "did not expect to get much more immersive feelings in VR than desktop 3D" for a familiar scene, valued VR for stakeholder communication, but found gestures less precise than mouse (verified) — [arXiv 2107.00227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00227)
- **Web urban digital twin usability (Wang et al., n = 10 experts)**: key problems in data navigation, interface clarity and analytical flexibility; low UEQ perspicuity (0.75) vs high attractiveness (1.67); regional differences in preferences (units, seasonal analysis) (verified) — [Wang et al. 2025 PDF](https://ual.sg/publication/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces.pdf)
- **Navigation in urban VR**: a 2013 ISPRS study of a low-cost HMD vs desktop vs screen-projected setup (n = 18) found best performance on desktop, HMD "not dramatically worse"; a 70-participant pedestrian wayfinding study found better wayfinding performance in desktop VR than HMD VR with similar user experience (search-result summaries; full texts not read) — [ISPRS Archives XL-2-W2, 2013](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-2-W2/145/2013/); TopoIVE study with 60 participants (navigation vs free exploration groups) on 2D vs 3D urban representation — [Halik & Kent, Canterbury repository](https://repository.canterbury.ac.uk/download/a138586300a9867414292f89e08e606429e10af647d8a43e17fe064feb2bb3e3/2562970/Halik%20and%20Kent%20Paper%20R2.pdf)
- **Collaboration**: Ens et al. C14 ("Assessing Collaborative Work") states collaborative IA is hard to evaluate and needs methods accounting for differences between virtual and co-located work; Batch et al. (2020, IEEE TVCG, "There Is No Spoon: Evaluating Performance, Space Use, and Presence with Expert Domain Users in Immersive Analytics") is the reference for expert-user, presence and space-use evaluation (title from Ens et al. reference list, verified) — [Ens et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10290646)

### Inferences
- Expect for Helsingborg: extruded columns/height surfaces to beat colour-only on value-comparison accuracy but cost time and cause occlusion among 44,000 buildings + columns; inset columns and tilt/overview camera presets are the analogue of Tilt Map's mitigation. A task where buildings hide columns is a legitimate "occlusion stress" condition.
- Hypotheses a thesis can plausibly test: H1 extrusion more accurate than choropleth for compare/estimate tasks; H2 choropleth faster; H3 VR not significantly different in accuracy from desktop but higher SUS/preference; H4 VR more head/camera movement; H5 combined-variable tasks low accuracy in both.
- "Map on a table" (exocentric, scaled miniature in front of a seated user) is what Tilt Map, Kraus's VR miniature condition, and Wagner Filho's desk-coupled cube used; it is lower-risk for cybersickness and interaction precision than room-scale flying over a 70 km² city — a good default for the Quest 3S mode.

- Full text of the egocentric vs exocentric map navigation studies (e.g., Satriadi et al.) and of Yang et al. 2018 results was not read; only their summaries via Ens et al. and search snippets.
- Collaborative IA evaluation numbers not gathered (out of feasible scope for a single-user thesis).

## 5. Recommendations from IA surveys / grand-challenges papers on evaluation

### Takeaway
Grand-challenge and survey papers converge on: go beyond time/error (UX, workload, physiology), avoid student-only short studies where possible, account for novelty bias and learning effects, compare against a fair desktop baseline, specify conditions/tasks well enough for replication, and consider both controlled and in-the-wild studies. They explicitly say no standard IA evaluation framework or benchmark exists yet.

### Cited Findings
- Ens et al. (2021), "Grand Challenges in Immersive Analytics", CHI '21, doi:10.1145/3411764.3446866 — 17 challenges by 24 experts in 4 themes; evaluation-relevant ones: **C15 Defining Application Scenarios** (which scenarios/when in the workflow IA helps), **C16 Understanding Users and Contexts for Evaluation** (criticizes short studies with easily accessible students/colleagues; novelty bias; nausea/fatigue; need for long-term assessment and formal participant-selection protocols), **C17 Establishing an Evaluation Framework** (no grounded framework of methods/metrics; should include physiological measures like fatigue/arousal; guidance on when to run controlled vs "in the wild" studies; "curse of dimensionality" of factors), and **C14 Assessing Collaborative Work** (verified) — [Ens et al. 2021 (NSF PAR PDF)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10290646); [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3446866)
- Kraus et al. (2022) survey conclusions: results are "often quite specific to the conditions and tasks used", so better characterization/specification is needed for replication; no structured exploration of the design space; technology progress may "overwrite evaluation results of previous studies with outdated technologies"; suggests re-evaluating visualizations "with a bad reputation, such as 3D bar charts in immersive environments" and combining 2D and 3D with smooth transitions (verified) — [Kraus et al. 2022](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14430)
- Kraus et al. note immersion level depends on multisensory stimulation, resolution and fidelity, which can influence analysis tasks — i.e., hardware must be reported (verified) — [Kraus et al. 2022](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14430)
- Marriott et al. (eds.) (2018), *Immersive Analytics*, Springer LNCS 11190 — foundational book; Ens et al. cite it as calling for studies on the not-fully-understood trade-offs of immersive data display (verified via Ens et al.) — [doi:10.1007/978-3-030-01388-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-01388-2) (DOI from prior knowledge)
- Fonnet & Prié (2021), "Survey of Immersive Analytics", IEEE TVCG 27(3) — the "2019 survey" Ens et al. cite as extensively summarising the field (DOI from prior knowledge; full text not read this session) — [doi:10.1109/TVCG.2019.2929033](https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2019.2929033)

### Inferences
- A master thesis can address C16/C17 cheaply by: (1) including a small expert/domain sample alongside students; (2) reporting VR experience and analysing it as a covariate; (3) giving VR-specific training to reduce unfamiliarity confound; (4) measuring workload and sickness, not only time/error; (5) publishing task definitions, data versions, and logs (aligns with this repo's reproducibility rules).
- Frame the thesis contribution as an application-scenario study (C15): which urban analytic tasks on grid statistics benefit from VR vs desktop.

- Did not access Fonnet & Prié's evaluation statistics (e.g., share of papers with user studies) or the Marriott et al. book chapter on evaluation; these could add numbers but were not verified.

## 6. Real vs synthetic vs "seeded" data in IA evaluation

### Takeaway
Controlled geo-IA studies commonly use **real geography with generated (synthetic) attribute values** that preserve realistic statistical properties (e.g., spatial autocorrelation), transformed so answers are not trivial and spread across controlled ranges; this gives known ground truth, prevents memorization across trials, and removes prior-knowledge effects. Exploratory/usability sessions with domain users use real data. For a system-usability thesis, real Helsingborg geometry with either real SCB values (for exploration) or seeded/perturbed values (for benchmark tasks) is well-precedented.

### Cited Findings
- Tilt Map used real geographies (48 conterminous US states, 391 UK Local Authority Districts; EU NUTS-1 in Study 2) but **generated different data for each question** based on real population density, preserving **Moran's I** (spatial autocorrelation) of the original data; applied square-root (Study 1) or fourth-root (Study 2) transformations because the original skewed distributions "would have made it trivial" to answer; controlled correct answers to fixed ranges (verified) — [arXiv 2006.14120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14120)
- Besançon et al. used **real** French département population and unemployment data, with equivalent question sets rotated to avoid learning effects (verified) — [OSF osf.io/8rxwg](https://osf.io/8rxwg/)
- Kraus et al. survey: in network-visualization IA studies "authors resort to artificially created network data to conduct controlled user" studies (verified) — [Kraus et al. 2022](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14430)
- Wagner Filho et al. used constructed trajectory scenarios with two density levels (simple vs dense) to manipulate clutter as an experimental factor (verified) — [arXiv 1908.00580](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00580)
- Wang et al. and UrbanVR used real urban datasets for domain/expert sessions (real thermal comfort data; real site models) (verified) — [Wang et al. 2025](https://ual.sg/publication/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces/2025-cisbat-dt-interfaces.pdf); [arXiv 2107.00227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00227)

### Inferences
- Recommended approach for Helsingborg: keep the real city geometry and grid (ecological validity, familiar landmarks for local participants) and for **benchmark trials** use a "seeded" layer per trial: real SCB values perturbed or regenerated with preserved spatial autocorrelation, plus planted targets (one clear extreme cell, a controlled difference between two cells, a region with known mean). Store the seed, generation script, and ground-truth answer per trial under `Processed_data/` with a config, so every trial is reproducible (matches the repo's data-lineage rules).
- Tell participants explicitly that values in benchmark tasks are scenario values, not real statistics, to avoid prior-knowledge effects and ethical issues with displaying perturbed real socioeconomic/election data about identifiable neighbourhoods; use real values only in the free-exploration phase.
- Because the thesis targets system usability, not data accuracy, imperfect real data does not threaten validity as long as ground truth for each task is computed from exactly the values displayed.
- Local participants' prior knowledge of Helsingborg is a confound (they may "know" rich areas); seeded data neutralizes it; record familiarity with Helsingborg as a covariate.

### Gaps
- No IA paper found in this session that explicitly discusses ethics of perturbing real socioeconomic grid data; the recommendation above is an inference.
- The VAST Challenge practice of synthetic datasets with planted ground truth is a well-known precedent but was not verified in this session.
